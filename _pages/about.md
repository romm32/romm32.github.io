I am a third-year Ph.D. student in Electrical and Systems Engineering at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://alelab.seas.upenn.edu), advised by [Prof. Alejandro Ribeiro](https://alelab.seas.upenn.edu/Alejandro-Ribeiro/) in the [Alelab](https://alelab.seas.upenn.edu). My early Ph.D. research focused on resource allocation in wireless systems and decentralized collaboration in robot swarms—two topics I aim to bridge for my dissertation.

Originally from Montevideo, Uruguay, I earned my B.Sc. in Communication Systems Engineering from [Universidad de la República](https://udelar.edu.uy). As an undergraduate, I spent three years as a teaching and research assistant in the [ARTES](https://iie.fing.edu.uy/investigacion/grupos/artes/en/home/) group at the [Electrical Engineering Institute](https://iie.fing.edu.uy/en/welcome/). Working with [Prof. Federico La Rocca](https://iie.fing.edu.uy/personal/flarroca/blog/) on mobile network infrastructure algorithms for robot swarms and Graph Neural Network transferability laid the foundation for my current research. On the hands-on side, my capstone project involved building a low-cost Automatic Identification System (AIS) transceiver station for ships, which my supervisor [Claudina Rattaro](https://uy.linkedin.com/in/claudina-rattaro-1301l1984c/es) and I used to teach first-year engineering students coding and data communication fundamentals. I maintain strong ties to Uruguay, collaborating with colleagues back home to mentor capstone projects and organize technical workshops, as well as an annual radiofrequency [Capture the Flag competition](https://ctf.idm.uy/en-vivo/).

I am deeply passionate about teaching and mentorship, especially through hands-on activities. My experience spans leading cyberbullying awareness workshops for high schoolers, teaching undergraduate and graduate courses in data networks, wireless communications, and machine learning, and running workshops for high school girls to help close the gender gap in Information and Communication Technologies (ICT). The material I use for these activities is available online for reproduction.
