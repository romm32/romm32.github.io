I am a third-year Ph.D. student in Electrical and Systems Engineering at the [University of Pennsylvania](https://alelab.seas.upenn.edu), advised by [Prof. Alejandro Ribeiro](https://alelab.seas.upenn.edu/Alejandro-Ribeiro/) in the [Alelab](https://alelab.seas.upenn.edu). My early Ph.D. research focused on resource allocation in wireless systems and decentralized collaboration in robot swarms—two topics I aim to bridge for my dissertation.

Back home in Montevideo, Uruguay, I earned my B.Sc. in Communication Systems Engineering from [Universidad de la República](https://udelar.edu.uy). My capstone project involved building a low-cost Automatic Identification System (AIS) transceiver station for ships, under the supervision of [Claudina Rattaro](https://uy.linkedin.com/in/claudina-rattaro-1301l1984c/es). As an undergraduate, I spent three years as a teaching and research assistant at the [Electrical Engineering Institute](https://iie.fing.edu.uy/en/welcome/), where I worked with [Prof. Federico La Rocca](https://iie.fing.edu.uy/personal/flarroca/blog/) on mobile ad-hoc networking. 

I am passionate about teaching and mentorship through hands-on activities. My experience includes teaching undergraduate and graduate courses in data networks, wireless communications, and machine learning, and running workshops for high school girls to help close the gender gap in Information and Communication Technologies (ICT). I also help organize an annual radiofrequency [Capture the Flag competition](https://ctf.idm.uy/en-vivo/).
